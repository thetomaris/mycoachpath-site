A Letter from Tom Aris, Program Author
Why Most Attempts Fail
There are over 500,000 leadership and executive coaches globally.
Experience alone no longer differentiates. Credentials no longer differentiate. “Leadership ” as positioning no longer differentiates.
Most former corporate leaders enter the market assuming credibility will translate into demand.
It does not.
In a crowded advisory market, authority must be defined, not implied.
Broad positioning disappears. Undefined messaging blends in. Executive-level clients look for specificity, not general leadership language.
Strong operators remain invisible. Not because they lack depth. But because they refuse to narrow.
The market is not too crowded. It is too undifferentiated.
This requires you to give up who you are not.
There are many competent professionals. There are far fewer defined characters. Many former corporate leaders default to institutional language. Measured tone. Safe phrasing. Abstract leadership vocabulary. Partly out of professionalism. Partly out of caution. You do not want to step on former corporate toes.
Imposter syndrome reinforces this restraint. So does the desire to remain broadly acceptable.
The result is polished neutrality. In a market this crowded, neutrality disappears. Authority does not come from sounding professional. It comes from standing somewhere specific. That means having edges. A defined point of view. A voice that is recognizably yours.
Tom Aris Founder & Program Author
